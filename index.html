Scholarly Prompt Lab

The prompt as method: design, document and evaluate AI-assisted humanities research.

Everything runs in your browser. Nothing you type is sent anywhere, and the Lab does not call any AI model: you copy the prompt into the system you are studying, then log what came back.

Use your own research

Type your own question and sources, or load your own text files (.txt, .md, .csv, transcripts). Files stay on your computer.

Prompt-sensitivity study

        
The same research task under four conditions. Each condition adds one layer, and every condition keeps the same output specification so the results stay comparable. Run each condition several times on each model, then log the outputs.

        
A fuller prompt may improve an output, introduce new errors, or simply teach the model to agree with you. The point is to find out which.

The CONTEXT framework

        
A proposed framework for situated and accountable scholarly prompting. Its claim is simple: when scholars delegate interpretive work to AI, the prompt becomes part of how knowledge is made, so it has to be designed, documented and checked like any other research instrument.

        

        
Four roles AI can play in humanities research

        

          
Research tool
Transcription, extraction, coding, organisation. Check accuracy against a hand-coded sample.

          
Object of study
Studying a model's assumptions and representations. The prompt is your experimental condition.

          
Interpretive aid
Exploring hypotheses and alternative readings. Every reading stays under scholarly judgement.

          
Research infrastructure
Search and retrieval over curated archives. Document what the archive leaves out.

        

        
The framework is offered for evaluation and refinement. See docs/CONTEXT-framework.md in the repository for the full guide.
