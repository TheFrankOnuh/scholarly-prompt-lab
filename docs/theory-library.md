# The theory library: how it was compiled and checked

The library holds 176 entries. Each has a type (theory, framework, approach, method, concept, school, movement or critique), one to three key works in Harvard author-date style, the framework's own core concepts, a statement of what the analysis should attend to and, for contested positions, well-known critiques.

## How citations were checked

Every citation was searched for in public library catalogues (Crossref, Open Library, the Internet Archive, Wikidata and OpenAlex) on 25 November 2026, matching title, first author and year.

| Result | Citations | How it is shown in the Lab |
|---|---|---|
| Confirmed: title, author and year | 383 | No mark |
| Title and author found; the catalogues do not list this edition's year (often a first edition or a translation) | 42 | Marked “edition year not confirmed” |
| Not found in the catalogues searched, kept because it is the entry's only key work | 2 | Marked “not found in catalogues” |

Catalogue coverage is uneven. Books from smaller publishers, including many African imprints, book chapters and reports are often missing. A mark therefore means “check this before citing”, not “this is wrong”. A catalogue match confirms that a work exists with that title, author and year; it does not confirm page ranges, and it does not judge the description of the framework.

**Before you cite any reference from the Lab, check it against your own copy.**

## Notes for specialist reviewers

These are the drafting notes on entries where a specialist's eye is most needed. Corrections are welcome: use “Report an error in this entry” in the Lab.


### Communication & media

- **Classical rhetoric** (`rhetoric-classical`): Ancient text (4th century BCE); the year given is that of Kennedy's widely used translation, so no integer original_year is supplied. The five canons are systematised in Roman rhetoric (Cicero, Quintilian), not by Aristotle.
- **Communication for development and social change** (`dev-comm`) · bibliographic details to confirm: A field rather than a single theory; the works given trace its paradigm shifts. Lerner (1958) and Schramm (1964) are the modernisation-era foundations and Freire (1970) the main source of the participatory turn; they could be added. Beltrán's title punctuation (serial comma) should be checked.
- **Counterpublics** (`counterpublics`): 'Subaltern counterpublics', 'weak/strong publics' and 'parallel discursive arenas' are Fraser's; 'stranger relationality' is Warner's.
- **Cultivation theory** (`cultivation`) · bibliographic details to confirm: Contested theory; debate included. Start page of Gerbner & Gross 1976 is cited variously as 172 or 173.
- **Diffusion of innovations** (`diffusion`): Five editions (1962 to 2003); first edition cited.
- **Dramatism** (`dramatism`): 'Terministic screens' comes from Burke's Language as Symbolic Action (1966), not cited here.
- **Encoding/decoding** (`encoding-decoding`) · bibliographic details to confirm: The 1980 chapter is an edited version of the 1973 CCCS stencilled paper 'Encoding and Decoding in the Television Discourse'. Page range 128–138 is the commonly cited one; worth checking against the printed volume.
- **Framing** (`framing`): Name shortened from 'Framing theory' to 'Framing', as the field usually writes it. 'Primary frameworks' and 'keying' are Goffman's terms; the remainder are Entman's.
- **Gatekeeping** (`gatekeeping`): The gatekeeper metaphor originates with Kurt Lewin (1947, 'Frontiers in group dynamics II', Human Relations); not cited here to keep the entry to verified communication-studies sources.
- **Information disorder** (`information-disorder`): Report, not peer-reviewed; may not resolve in Crossref but is indexed by the Council of Europe and Google Scholar. The report hyphenates the three terms.
- **Media ecology** (`media-ecology`): Postman is usually credited with naming the field (a 1968 NCTE address, published 1970) but that text is hard to cite precisely, so the canonical books are given instead. Ong's work is directly relevant to scholarship on African oral traditions; a specialist may wish to add a critique of the orality/literacy 'great divide' (e.g. Finnegan). Overlaps with medium-theory.
- **Mediatization** (`media-logic`) · bibliographic details to confirm: The id 'media-logic' is kept for link stability; media logic (Altheide & Snow) is the institutionalist strand within mediatization research. 'Mediatization' is kept in -z- spelling because the field uses it even in British English. Hjarvard 2008 page range should be checked (105–134).
- **Medium theory** (`medium-theory`): 'Medium theory' as a label is Meyrowitz's; Innis and McLuhan are its originators. Overlaps with media-ecology; the two are kept separate as the label and the school have distinct literatures.
- **Networked publics** (`networked-publics`): The four affordances listed are those of boyd 2010; the 2014 book revises them to persistence, visibility, spreadability and searchability. boyd writes her name in lower case.
- **Oramedia and indigenous communication** (`oramedia`) · bibliographic details to confirm: Oramedia is correctly attributed to Frank Okwu Ugboajah. Chapter pages and the publisher place (Hans Zell, Munich/London, 1985) should be checked against the printed book. Des Wilson's work on traditional communication systems in Nigeria (e.g. his Africa Media Review article of the late 1980s and his taxonomy of idiophones, membranophones, symbolography etc.) is central to this field but is deliberately not cited because the exact title, year and pages could not be confirmed; a specialist should add it.
- **Political economy of communication** (`political-economy-comm`) · bibliographic details to confirm: Socialist Register is an annual; vol. 10 (1973). 'Audience commodity' is Smythe's term (1977, Canadian Journal of Political and Social Theory), not cited here.
- **Public sphere** (`public-sphere`): Social Text numbers 25/26 were a combined issue without separate volume and issue numbers.
- **Ritual view of communication** (`ritual-communication`) · bibliographic details to confirm: The 1975 article is reprinted as chapter 1 of the 1989 book. Journal details of the 1975 article (Communication 2(1): 1–22) should be checked.
- **Spiral of silence** (`spiral-silence`): The English book subtitle is printed with a dash ('Public Opinion – Our Social Skin'); punctuation may vary by catalogue.
- **Uses and gratifications** (`uses-gratifications`): 'Gratifications sought/obtained' is a later refinement (Palmgreen and colleagues) and is not used here.

### Language & discourse

- **Appraisal theory** (`appraisal`): Developed within systemic functional linguistics.
- **Code-switching** (`code-switching`): New entry created from the split of 'translanguaging'. Myers-Scotton's Social Motivations draws mainly on Kenyan (Swahili/English) data, making it directly relevant to African multilingualism, including English, Nigerian Pidgin, Igbo, Yoruba and Hausa.
- **Conceptual metaphor theory** (`metaphor`): 'Source domain' and 'target domain' are the standard terms from later work (Lakoff 1993); Lakoff & Johnson 1980 mainly speak of structuring one concept in terms of another.
- **Conversational implicature** (`implicature`): Renamed from 'Pragmatics and implicature': pragmatics is a whole subfield, while the entry describes Grice's theory specifically.
- **Critical discourse analysis (dialectical-relational approach)** (`cda`): Split as required: this id now covers Fairclough's dialectical-relational approach only. Van Dijk is moved to cda-sociocognitive; Wodak's approach is the existing 'dha' entry and van Leeuwen's the existing 'social-actors' entry. The label 'dialectical-relational' is from Fairclough's chapter in Wodak & Meyer (eds), Methods of Critical Discourse Analysis, 2nd edn (2009).
- **Critical discourse analysis (sociocognitive approach)** (`cda-sociocognitive`): New entry created from the split of 'cda'.
- **Discourse-historical approach** (`dha`): English translation of the 1998 German original (Zur diskursiven Konstruktion nationaler Identität).
- **Ethnography of communication** (`ethnography-communication`) · bibliographic details to confirm: Page range of the 1962 chapter should be checked.
- **Language ideologies** (`language-ideology`) · bibliographic details to confirm: Irvine and Gal spell the terms 'iconization'; British spelling used per house rule. Irvine & Gal draw partly on Senegalese (Wolof) data. Silverstein chapter pages should be checked.
- **Linguistic relativity** (`linguistic-relativity`): 'Sapir–Whorf hypothesis' is a later label not used by either author; avoided here. Whorf's collection was first issued by the Technology Press of MIT.
- **Multimodal discourse analysis** (`multimodal`): This entry covers the social semiotic strand. The systemic-functional strand (O'Halloran and colleagues) is distinct. 'Framing' here is Kress and van Leeuwen's compositional term, not media framing.
- **Politeness theory** (`politeness`) · bibliographic details to confirm: The 1978 original appeared as a long chapter in Goody (ed.), Questions and Politeness. The concept of face comes from Goffman (1967, 'On face-work'). Nwoye 1992 is especially relevant to a Nigerian audience; please check its details.
- **Speech act theory** (`speech-acts`) · bibliographic details to confirm: Derrida chapter is in his own collection (translated by Alan Bass), hence no editors. Medium confidence applies to the Derrida page range only.
- **Translanguaging** (`translanguaging`): Split from 'Code-switching and translanguaging': translanguaging theory explicitly rejects the separate-codes premise of code-switching research, so the two cannot share an entry. Code-switching is the new entry 'code-switching'. The term originates in Cen Williams's 1994 Welsh doctoral thesis (trawsieithu), not cited.

### Literature, orality & translation

- **Bolekaja criticism** (`bolekaja`) · bibliographic details to confirm: Renamed from 'Decolonisation of African literature' to the name the position is known by; 'bolekaja' (Yoruba, 'come down, let's fight') is the authors' own label. Reprinted Washington, DC: Howard University Press, 1983 and London: KPI, 1985. Check exact subtitle wording and Transition pagination.
- **Deconstruction** (`deconstruction`): The Derrida–Searle exchange (Glyph, 1977) is the best-known debate but is omitted because Glyph is hard to verify automatically.
- **Dialogism and heteroglossia** (`dialogism`): Problems of Dostoevsky's Poetics first appeared in 1929 as Problems of Dostoevsky's Art; the 1963 revised edition is the basis of the English translation. The chronotope is a distinct Bakhtinian concept and is deliberately not merged here.
- **Domestication and foreignisation** (`foreignisation`) · bibliographic details to confirm: Venuti builds on Schleiermacher's 1813 lecture on the methods of translating. Check the Tymoczko citation details.
- **Ecocriticism** (`ecocriticism`): Nixon's postcolonial ecocriticism (including Ken Saro-Wiwa and the Niger Delta) is a distinct strand from the American nature-writing focus of early ecocriticism.
- **Intertextuality** (`intertextuality`): Kristeva coined the term in 'Word, dialogue and novel' (written 1966, published in Séméiotikè, 1969), translated in Desire in Language. She later preferred 'transposition' (Revolution in Poetic Language).
- **Narratology** (`narratology`): Genette's Narrative Discourse translates the 'Discours du récit' section of Figures III (1972). Bal's Dutch original is De theorie van vertellen en verhalen (1978); her revision of Genette's focalisation is itself a key internal debate.
- **New Historicism** (`new-historicism`): Greenblatt prefers 'cultural poetics'. Montrose's phrase on the historicity of texts appears in his essay in the Veeser volume, which also contains critiques of the approach.
- **Oral literature in Africa** (`oral-literature-africa`): Finnegan 1970 appeared in the Oxford Library of African Literature; an open-access second edition was published by Open Book Publishers in 2012.
- **Oral-formulaic theory** (`oral-formulaic`): Parry's papers date from 1928 to 1935 (beginning with his French theses); the 1971 collection gathers them in English, so no single original_year is given.
- **Orality and literacy** (`orality-literacy`): Contested 'great divide' thesis; Finnegan's Literacy and Orality (1988) is a further critique relevant to African material.
- **Orature** (`orature`) · bibliographic details to confirm: The term was coined by the Ugandan linguist Pio Zirimu (Makerere, late 1960s); no published work by Zirimu defining it can be cited with certainty, so he appears only in 't'. Ngũgĩ credits Zirimu and popularised the term, also in Decolonising the Mind (1986). Check details of the 2007 Performance Research article.
- **Reader-response criticism** (`reader-response`) · bibliographic details to confirm: Renamed to the usual Anglophone label. Iser belongs to the Konstanz school of reception aesthetics (Rezeptionsästhetik, with Jauss), which is distinct from American reader-response criticism; a reviewer may prefer to split them. Check the Diacritics pagination.
- **Signifyin(g)** (`signifying`): Gates spells the Yorùbá orisha 'Esu-Elegbara' without diacritics; the concept list keeps his spelling and 'a' uses standard Yorùbá orthography.
- **Skopos theory** (`skopos`) · bibliographic details to confirm: German original: Grundlegung einer allgemeinen Translationstheorie (Tübingen: Niemeyer, 1984). The English translation is also listed under Routledge (2013/2014) after St. Jerome's acquisition; verify imprint and year.
- **Structuralism** (`structuralism`): Image Music Text collects Barthes essays including 'Introduction to the structural analysis of narratives' (1966); no single original_year applies. Culler's Structuralist Poetics (1975) could be added as the literary-critical synthesis.
- **World literature** (`world-literature`): Casanova's original is La République mondiale des lettres (Paris: Seuil, 1999). Moretti's 'Conjectures on world literature' (New Left Review, 2000) is a third founding text.

### History & memory

- **Annales school and the longue durée** (`annales`) · bibliographic details to confirm: Braudel's Mediterranean appeared in English in two volumes (1972–1973; also New York: Harper & Row). On History translates Écrits sur l'histoire (1969) and contains the 1958 longue durée essay. The 1949 French edition was substantially revised in 1966; the translation follows the second edition.
- **Collective memory** (`collective-memory`): On Collective Memory translates mainly Les cadres sociaux de la mémoire (1925) plus part of La topographie légendaire des évangiles (1941). The Collective Memory translates the posthumous La mémoire collective (1950).
- **Conceptual history (Begriffsgeschichte)** (`conceptual-history`): Name adds the German term by which the approach is known. German original: Vergangene Zukunft (Frankfurt: Suhrkamp, 1979). The Geschichtliche Grundbegriffe lexicon (1972–1997, with Brunner and Conze) is the approach's main collective work. Comparison with Skinner's Cambridge School is a standard debate.
- **Gender as a category of historical analysis** (`gender-history`) · bibliographic details to confirm: Check the Downs citation details. For African settings, reviewers may wish to cross-reference Oyěwùmí's challenge to gender as a universal category (gender field).
- **History from below** (`history-below`): Thompson's short 1966 Times Literary Supplement piece 'History from below' gave the approach its name but is omitted as hard to verify automatically.
- **Ibadan School of history** (`ibadan-school`): Name simplified to the usual form. Ajayi's book appeared in the Ibadan History Series; check whether the subtitle uses 'Élite' with an accent. Ajayi's 'colonial episode' argument appears in his essays of the late 1960s, not cited here.
- **Invention of tradition** (`invented-tradition`) · bibliographic details to confirm: Check pagination of Ranger 1993. Spear's 'Neo-traditionalism and the limits of invention in British colonial Africa' (Journal of African History, 2003) is a further critique.
- **Microhistory** (`microhistory`): Italian original: Il formaggio e i vermi (Turin: Einaudi, 1976). 'The exceptional normal' (eccezionale normale) is Edoardo Grendi's phrase. Giovanni Levi's 'On microhistory' (1991) is the other standard statement.
- **Oral tradition as history** (`oral-tradition`): French original: De la tradition orale: essai de méthode historique (Tervuren: Musée Royal de l'Afrique Centrale, 1961). Vansina distinguishes oral tradition (transmitted across generations) from oral history (eyewitness reminiscence).
- **Reading along the archival grain** (`archival-grain`): Stoler positions reading along the grain as a complement to, not a replacement for, reading against it.
- **Silencing the past** (`silencing-past`): The Haitian Revolution as 'unthinkable' is Trouillot's central case.
- **Sites of memory (lieux de mémoire)** (`lieux-memoire`): Translated by Marc Roudebush from Nora's introduction to Les Lieux de mémoire, vol. 1 (1984). The full English abridgement is Realms of Memory (Columbia University Press, 1996–1998).
- **Subaltern studies** (`subaltern`) · bibliographic details to confirm: Check pagination of Guha's opening statement in Subaltern Studies I.

### African, postcolonial & decolonial

- **Afrocentricity** (`afrocentricity`): Contested position; both debates included.
- **Can the subaltern speak?** (`subaltern-speak`) · bibliographic details to confirm: The 1988 chapter was co-published by Macmillan Education (Basingstoke) and University of Illinois Press (Urbana). Spivak substantially revised the essay in chapter 3 of the 1999 book. Parry citation details should be checked.
- **Coloniality of power and knowledge** (`coloniality`): Ndlovu-Gatsheni added as the principal African elaboration of the decolonial school.
- **Conceptual decolonisation** (`conceptual-decolonisation`): Wiredu's fullest programmatic statement is 'Toward decolonizing African philosophy and religion', African Studies Quarterly 1(4), 1998; not cited because the page range was not verified.
- **Critique of ethnophilosophy** (`ethnophilosophy-critique`) · bibliographic details to confirm: English translation of Sur la 'philosophie africaine' (Maspero, 1976), with an introduction by Abiola Irele. Owomoyela pages should be checked. The ethnophilosophy debate is one of the most contested in African philosophy; the critique is not a consensus position.
- **Decolonising the mind** (`decolonising-mind`): Co-published by James Currey (London), Heinemann Kenya (Nairobi) and Heinemann (Portsmouth, NH). The language debate also goes back to Obiajunwa Wali, 'The dead end of African literature?', Transition 10 (1963); not cited because page range not verified.
- **Hybridity and the Third Space** (`hybridity`) · bibliographic details to confirm: October 28 (Spring 1984) has no issue number distinct from the volume; issue left blank. Parry's volume/issue (9:1–2) should be checked.
- **Ibuanyidanda (complementary reflection)** (`ibuanyidanda`) · bibliographic details to confirm: The 2007 LIT Verlag volume (series Studies in African Philosophy) is the canonical statement of ibuanyidanda; exact subtitle punctuation should be checked against the title page. The 2004 Calabar volume is a regional imprint that automated checks may not find.
- **Indigenous knowledge systems** (`iks`) · bibliographic details to confirm: Odora Hoppers 2002 is published in South Africa (Claremont, Cape Town); exact subtitle should be checked. Agrawal is high confidence.
- **Indigenous research methodologies** (`indigenous-methods`): 'Relational accountability' is usually credited to Shawn Wilson, Research Is Ceremony (Fernwood, 2008), and is used by Chilisa. Smith 1999 co-published by University of Otago Press, Dunedin.
- **Necropolitics** (`necropolitics`): New entry split from postcolony. Article translated by Libby Meintjes. Mbembe's later book Necropolitics (Duke University Press, 2019) expands it.
- **Négritude** (`negritude`): Senghor's anthology carries Sartre's preface 'Orphée noir', source of the phrase 'anti-racist racism'. Damas's Pigments (Paris: Guy Lévi Mano, 1937) is the third founding text; not cited to keep within three works.
- **Orientalism** (`orientalism`): Renamed from 'Orientalism and othering'. 'Othering' as a term is usually credited to Spivak (1985, 'The Rani of Sirmur'), not Said, so it was removed from the name to avoid misattribution.
- **Pan-Africanism** (`pan-africanism`): Esedebe (Nigerian historian) added as the standard intellectual history of the movement.
- **Personhood and communitarianism** (`communitarian-personhood`) · bibliographic details to confirm: Menkiti and Gyekye hold opposed positions within one debate; kept as one entry with Gyekye as the debate. Menkiti page range (171–181) in the 3rd edition of Wright's volume should be checked.
- **Postcolonial theory** (`postcolonial`): Umbrella entry. Said (orientalism), Bhabha (hybridity) and Spivak (subaltern-speak) have their own entries, so canonical works here are field-defining overviews rather than their monographs. Type set to school because it names a tradition rather than a single theory.
- **Sage philosophy** (`sage-philosophy`) · bibliographic details to confirm: Bodunrin's assessment of sagacity should be confirmed against the article; the citation itself is believed correct.
- **The colonial library** (`colonial-library`): Renamed from 'The invention of Africa' to name the analytical concept rather than the book title.
- **The postcolony** (`postcolony`): Split: necropolitics is a distinct later concept and now has its own entry (necropolitics). On the Postcolony translates De la postcolonie (Karthala, 2000).
- **Ubuntu** (`ubuntu`): Split. This entry is Ramose's ontological and epistemological account; Metz's normative moral theory is now ubuntu-moral-theory. Revised edition 2002 (Mond Books).
- **Ubuntu as moral theory** (`ubuntu-moral-theory`) · bibliographic details to confirm: New entry split from ubuntu. Metz 2007 is high confidence; the AHRLJ 2011 page range should be checked.
- **Underdevelopment** (`underdevelopment`): Rodney 1972 co-published with Tanzania Publishing House, Dar es Salaam. Warren 1980 appeared under the NLB/Verso imprint.

### Gender, race & intersectionality

- **Critical race theory** (`crt`): 'Whiteness as property' is Cheryl Harris, Harvard Law Review 106(8), 1993.
- **Feminist standpoint theory** (`standpoint`): Renamed to 'Feminist standpoint theory', the name used in the field. 'Strong objectivity' is from Harding's Whose Science? Whose Knowledge? (Cornell University Press, 1991).
- **Hegemonic masculinity** (`masculinities`): The concept first appears in Carrigan, Connell and Lee, 'Toward a new sociology of masculinity', Theory and Society 14(5), 1985.
- **Intersectionality** (`intersectionality`): Collins's matrix of domination is covered in black-feminist.
- **Male daughters, female husbands** (`amadiume`): 'Dual-sex political system' in the old entry is Kamene Okonjo's concept (in Hafkin and Bay (eds), Women in Africa, Stanford University Press, 1976), not Amadiume's, and was removed from this entry's terms. Amadiume extends the argument in Reinventing Africa: Matriarchy, Religion and Culture (Zed Books, 1997).
- **Motherism** (`motherism`) · bibliographic details to confirm: Acholonu 1995 is a Nigerian imprint (Owerri: Afa Publications, Women in Environmental Development Series) that automated databases may not index; it should be preserved manually if not verified. Nkealah 2016 details should be checked. Contested position; debate included.
- **Postcolonial feminism** (`postcolonial-feminism`) · bibliographic details to confirm: The 1984 boundary 2 issue is a double issue (vol. 12 no. 3 / vol. 13 no. 1); revised version in Feminist Review 30 (1988). Volume/issue formatting may need adjusting for automated checking.
- **Snail-sense feminism** (`snail-sense`) · bibliographic details to confirm: Delivered as a University of Lagos inaugural lecture and published in its Monograph Series (No. 17) by Wealthsmith Books, Lagos, 2012. Imprint details should be checked; automated databases may not index it, so it should be preserved manually if not verified.
- **Stiwanism** (`stiwanism`): The term is introduced in the chapter 'Stiwanism: feminism in an African context' in this collection; 'six mountains' is from the earlier essay 'African women, culture and another development', reprinted there.
- **The invention of women** (`invention-women`) · bibliographic details to confirm: Highly contested thesis. The best-known critique is Bibi Bakare-Yusuf, '"Yorubas don't do gender": a critical review of Oyeronke Oyewumi's The Invention of Women', which appeared in the CODESRIA volume African Gender Scholarship: Concepts, Methodologies and Paradigms (Dakar: CODESRIA, 2004, Gender Series 1). Not cited formally because the editors and page range could not be confirmed with certainty; it argues that Oyěwùmí overstates the primacy of language and seniority and neglects embodied, lived differences. J. Lorand Matory has also criticised the thesis. Peel 2002 details should be confirmed.

### Philosophy & ethics

- **Capabilities approach** (`capabilities`): Development as Freedom also appeared from Knopf, New York, in 1999.
- **Critical theory (Frankfurt School)** (`critical-theory`): Dialectic of Enlightenment circulated in mimeograph in 1944 as Philosophische Fragmente; the first published edition is Querido, Amsterdam, 1947. Horkheimer's 1937 essay 'Traditional and Critical Theory' is the programmatic statement but is not cited because I could not confirm page numbers in the 1972 English collection.
- **Ethics of care** (`care-ethics`): Gilligan is developmental psychology; Noddings and Tronto develop the ethical and political theory. Tronto's phases of care (caring about, taking care of, care-giving, care-receiving) map to attentiveness, responsibility, competence and responsiveness.
- **Existentialism** (`existentialism`): Fanon removed from the thinkers list: he engages Sartre critically and has his own entry (id fanon). De Beauvoir's The Second Sex belongs with gender theory and is not cited here.
- **Fanon's critique of colonialism** (`fanon`): Renamed from 'Psychology of colonialism', which understates Fanon's political theory of decolonisation. Fanon is often read with existential phenomenology and psychiatry; specialist may prefer field afr. Note the 2008 Philcox translations (Grove) are now widely used.
- **Phenomenology** (`phenomenology`): Husserl's transcendental phenomenology and Merleau-Ponty's existential phenomenology of embodiment are distinct strands of one tradition. 'Lifeworld' (Lebenswelt) is from Husserl's later Crisis and is not cited here.
- **Philosophical hermeneutics** (`hermeneutics`): Gadamer and Ricoeur are kept together as the standard pairing for philosophical hermeneutics, though Ricoeur's 'hermeneutics of suspicion' and his theory of distanciation depart from Gadamer. The Habermas-Gadamer debate is the major dispute; no citation given because I could not pin an English source to exact page numbers.
- **Power/knowledge and discourse** (`power-knowledge`): Power/Knowledge (1980) is an English-language compilation with no single French original, hence no original_year. US edition: Pantheon, New York.
- **Pragmatism** (`pragmatism`): Popular Science Monthly in 1878 was not numbered by issue; Peirce's article appeared in the January 1878 number.
- **Virtue ethics** (`virtue-ethics`): Aristotle is cited in Irwin's translation; no original_year is given for an ancient text (composed 4th century BCE). Anscombe added as the paper usually credited with reviving virtue ethics in analytic philosophy.

### Sociology, anthropology & cultural studies

- **Cultural hegemony** (`hegemony`): The notebooks were written 1929 to 1935 and first published in Italian 1948 to 1951 (Einaudi); original_year omitted because the English selection draws on unpublished manuscripts and the later critical edition (1975).
- **Cultural materialism** (`cultural-materialism`): Not to be confused with Marvin Harris's anthropological 'cultural materialism' (1979). In literary studies the label is also used for Dollimore and Sinfield's British Shakespeare criticism, which derives from Williams.
- **Dramaturgy and the presentation of self** (`dramaturgy`): First issued in 1956 as a monograph by the University of Edinburgh Social Sciences Research Centre; the expanded 1959 Doubleday edition is the standard citation. Goffman writes 'front region' and 'back region'; 'front stage' is a later gloss.
- **Field, habitus and capital** (`bourdieu`): Outline (1977) is a substantially revised version of Esquisse d'une théorie de la pratique (1972). Distinction also published by Harvard University Press, Cambridge, MA, 1984.
- **Historical materialism** (`historical-materialism`) · bibliographic details to confirm: The German Ideology was written 1845 to 1846 and first published in full in 1932. The 1859 Preface to the Contribution contains the classic summary of historical materialism. Medium confidence only on the exact publisher wording of the 1904 Kerr edition. Engels added to thinkers as co-author.
- **Imagined communities** (`imagined-communities`): Revised edition 1991 added chapters on census, map and museum.
- **Interpretive anthropology** (`thick-description`): Geertz borrowed 'thick description' from Gilbert Ryle.
- **Legitimate authority** (`weber-authority`): Weber's Herrschaft is translated as both 'authority' and 'domination'. 'Politics as a vocation' was delivered as a lecture in 1919. Weber's patrimonialism underpins later Africanist work on neopatrimonialism.
- **Moral panics** (`moral-panic`): Hall et al., Policing the Crisis (1978), extends the concept to hegemony and the state; thinkers list mentions it but it is not cited as a work. Change t to 'Cohen' if preferred.
- **Ritual process and liminality** (`liminality`): Turner's theory rests on fieldwork among the Ndembu of Zambia. Van Gennep added as the originator of the tripartite schema.
- **Social capital** (`social-capital`): Three distinct traditions share the term: Bourdieu's (relational, unequal access, covered in id bourdieu), Coleman's (rational action and closure) and Putnam's (civic and aggregate). A specialist may want these split; I kept one entry because Bourdieu already has his own.
- **Social identity theory** (`social-identity`): Self-categorisation theory (Turner et al. 1987) is a later development and distinct.
- **Symbolic interactionism** (`symbolic-interactionism`): Mind, Self, and Society is compiled from students' lecture notes after Mead's death. Blumer coined the term 'symbolic interactionism' (1937).
