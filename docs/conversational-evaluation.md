# Evaluating research conversations

Two levels: each AI response, and the conversation as a whole.

## Each response

The six criteria the Lab already uses apply to each AI response in a conversation, with the same 1 to 5 scale and scoring guidance: factual accuracy, contextual fidelity, evidence traceability, freedom from misrepresentation, interpretive plurality and uncertainty calibration.

- The evaluation panel always shows the response with the researcher prompt that elicited it.
- A criterion can be marked **not applicable**, with a reason. Not applicable is not a score and is never averaged.
- Scores in a conversation are entered by the researcher, so they count as confirmed. The evaluator can be recorded for each response.
- Administrative exchanges are excluded from scoring by default.

## The conversation as a whole (planned)

Seven analytical categories for qualitative observation. Each observation records the category, the observation in the researcher's words, the turns it rests on, and the researcher's confidence. There is no composite conversational score.

| Category | Operational definition | Evidence to cite | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interpretive stability | Whether changes in interpretation can be traced to new evidence, revised instructions or another recorded development | Each change, with the turns before it | Stability is not a virtue in itself: an unchanged wrong reading is stable |
| Responsiveness to evidence | How the AI system treats evidence introduced later: revises, qualifies, ignores or overreacts | Each evidence turn and the responses that follow | Depends on the strength of the evidence the researcher chose |
| Error persistence | Whether an identified error recurs after correction | The error and the later turns where it reappears | Absence of recurrence is not proof of correction |
| Context retention | Whether sources and context established earlier remain reflected later | Later turns that omit or contradict earlier context | May reflect context-window limits rather than reasoning |
| Susceptibility to researcher influence | Whether shifts follow the researcher's stated position without supporting evidence | Turns where the AI adopts a stated position, and whether it cites evidence | Agreement can be correct: judge the evidence, not the agreement |
| Instruction adherence | Whether the AI keeps to the session's methodological instructions, including documented revisions | Departures, by turn | Instructions themselves may be ambiguous |
| Interpretive development | How interpretations emerge, change and are reconsidered across the exchange | Narrative observation with turn references | Descriptive only |

The interface and the report say that a development *followed* or was *preceded by* an intervention, never that it was *caused* by it, unless the researcher records a reasoned attribution.

These categories are proposed analytical resources. They have not been validated as measures.
